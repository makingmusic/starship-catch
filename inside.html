Ship 42 flew with no one aboard

A hundred people, in the volume they caught.

The ship is 52 metres tall and 9 metres across. SpaceX says a crew version of it can carry up to 100 people on a long flight, with private cabins, a common room, storage, a solar-storm shelter, and a viewing gallery. A lunar trip has also been described with about 12 passengers in private quarters. No seat map has been published.

So this page does not invent one. It puts things you already know into the published volume: a 747 cabin, an ordinary economy seat, a Crew Dragon, and you. Click into the ship. Each step gets closer, down to the straps and the wall.

What is published

The cabin is described. The seat map is not.

SpaceX, on the crew ship

  • The vehicle page says Starship can carry up to 100 people on long interplanetary flights, and that a tanker is the same ship without the windows. The ship is 52 m tall and 9 m across.
  • The Starship Users Guide describes a crew ship for up to 100 people, with private cabins, large common areas, centralized storage, solar storm shelters, and a viewing gallery. Its payload envelope is 8 m in diameter, and an extended bay is up to 22 m tall.
  • A cylinder of that envelope is about 1,100 cubic metres. SpaceX has described the usable volume as about 1,000 cubic metres, including on its human-spaceflight page, where a lunar flight was listed at up to 12 passengers with private quarters.

The comparisons

  • Boeing’s 747-8 Intercontinental is 76.3 m long, with a cabin 6.1 m wide, and 467 seats in Boeing’s three-class reference. The main-deck ceiling is about 2.4 m on Boeing’s cabin drawings. Economy on that airplane is often 10 across, a seat about 46 cm wide and 79 cm from one seatback to the next.
  • Crew Dragon, on SpaceX’s vehicle page, is 4 m across and 9.3 cubic metres inside, and can carry up to 7 people. NASA flights use four seats.
  • Two 747 cabin cross-sections, stacked, fit inside the 8 m circle. That is geometry, not a SpaceX interior.

This drawing, on purpose

  • The deck spacing, the 5-4-5 seat grid, the nine cabins, the harness, and the person-sized window are a sketch so the published numbers have a body. SpaceX has not shown that hardware.
  • The person in the drawings is an illustration, scaled to the height in the header. It is not a photograph of a passenger. The 747 and the Crew Dragon beside the ship are illustrations too, scaled to 76.3 m and to 8.1 m with the trunk. The 4 m circle is the published diameter.
  • During ascent, “down” is toward the engines. During entry the ship falls belly-first, so “down” swings toward the heat shield. A seat has to hold you for both. The mechanism is not published.
  • The windward wall is tile. The tile width, about 24 cm, is a photo measurement, not a SpaceX spec. Ship 42’s own page on this site is the catch, the engines, and the shield.
